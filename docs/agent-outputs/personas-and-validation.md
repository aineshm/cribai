## 1. Executive Summary & Persona Prioritization

### Market Context

The U.S. student housing market exceeds $9.5 billion, with 62% of students at four-year institutions living off campus. Average rent per bed nationally is $915-$1,017/month (Yardi Matrix, College House 2025-26 data), occupancy rates hit 95.1% for the 2025-26 academic year, and 48% of college students report housing insecurity. The market has high demand, opaque information, and significant friction -- exactly where a housing intelligence platform delivers outsized value.

### Recommended Launch Persona Sequence

| Priority | Persona | Rationale |
|----------|---------|-----------|
| 1 | Returning Upperclassman | Highest organic spread, knows pain points, will generate reviews and content that seed the flywheel |
| 2 | Mid-Lease Subletter | Acute urgency creates immediate activation; sublet marketplace is severely underserved |
| 3 | Transfer Student | Compressed timeline + no local network = high willingness to try new tools |
| 4 | International Student | Deep pain (no credit, no network), high lifetime value, tight community networks for viral spread |
| 5 | Incoming Freshman | Largest volume but lowest autonomy; reached through parents and orientation channels |
| 6 | Graduate Student | Smaller cohort, different needs (family, longer lease), but high-value long-term users |

**Flywheel logic:** Upperclassmen seed the Landlord Reputation System with reviews. Subletters activate the Marketplace. Together, they create the content and liquidity that draws in transfer students and international students who need both intelligence and marketplace access. Freshmen arrive last because they require institutional partnerships (orientation, housing offices) to reach at scale.

### Persona 1: The Incoming Freshman

**Name archetype:** "Nervous Nora" / "Freshman Fam"

#### Demographics
- **Age:** 17-19
- **Year:** Rising sophomore (searching spring of freshman year for first off-campus place)
- **Budget:** $600-$900/month (often parent-subsidized)
- **Living situation:** Currently in dorms; 1-3 friends forming a group to rent together
- **Financial literacy:** Low -- first time reading a lease, no credit history

#### Housing Journey (Timeline: Feb - Aug)

| Month | Step | Emotion |
|-------|------|---------|
| Feb | Realizes dorm contract ends; friends start talking about apartments | Anxiety, FOMO |
| Mar | Browses Facebook groups, asks upperclassman friends for recs | Overwhelmed by options |
| Mar-Apr | Parents get involved; family tours 3-5 places over spring break or virtually | Relief + conflict (parent vs. student priorities) |
| Apr-May | Compares 2-3 finalists on price, distance, "vibe" | Decision paralysis |
| May | Signs lease (often with parent co-signer); pays deposit | Excitement + sticker shock |
| Aug | Moves in; reality check on condition vs. listing photos | Disappointment or pleasant surprise |

#### Information Sources
- **Primary:** Friends/upperclassmen word-of-mouth, Instagram/TikTok apartment tours
- **Secondary:** University housing office website, Facebook housing groups
- **Tertiary:** Google Maps (distance check), Apartments.com/Zillow (browsing)
- **Trust hierarchy:** Friends > current tenants > university resources > landlord marketing

#### Pain Points (Ranked by Severity)

1. **Information asymmetry** -- "I had no idea what questions to ask or what a normal lease looks like"
2. **Misleading listings** -- Deceptive advertising photos outdated compared to actual property condition; students report "power surges at least once a day, and the windows are held up by wooden rods"
3. **No landlord track record** -- Zero way to verify if a landlord is responsive or neglectful before signing
4. **Hidden costs** -- Security deposits, utilities, renters insurance, parking, furniture; "Utilities and groceries are more expensive than many expect. There are upfront fees like the first month's rent, security deposits, cleaning supplies, furniture and even moving vans" (Olive Salas, Off-Campus Living peer navigator)
5. **Timing pressure** -- Best units gone by March; creates panic-signing

#### Decision Criteria (Ranked)
1. Price (parent-driven hard cap)
2. Proximity to campus
3. Friend group compatibility (want to live with specific people)
4. Safety/neighborhood feel (parent priority)
5. Amenities (laundry in-unit, Wi-Fi included)
6. Landlord reputation (would be higher if information existed)

#### CampusNest Value
**Primary pillar: Housing Intelligence Engine** -- Cost calculator showing true all-in monthly cost, comparable pricing, and fairness scoring directly address the information gap that freshmen and their parents face.

**Secondary pillar: Landlord Reputation System** -- Parents especially will value verified reviews. This is a "trust unlock" that differentiates CampusNest from generic listing sites.

#### Adoption Trigger
A friend shows them a CampusNest cost comparison during a group apartment discussion and says "look, this place is actually $200/month more than it seems once you add utilities and parking." The parent then bookmarks the site.

### Persona 2: The Returning Upperclassman

**Name archetype:** "Savvy Sam"

#### Demographics
- **Age:** 20-22
- **Year:** Junior or Senior
- **Budget:** $700-$1,100/month (mix of parental support, part-time work, loans)
- **Living situation:** Currently in off-campus apartment; may be renewing or searching for better deal
- **Financial literacy:** Moderate -- has signed at least one lease before

#### Housing Journey (Timeline: Nov - Mar)

| Month | Step | Emotion |
|-------|------|---------|
| Nov | Receives renewal notice from current landlord with rent increase | Frustration |
| Dec | Decides to stay or search; polls friend group | Strategic calculation |
| Jan | Actively searches; leverages personal network for leads | Confidence + impatience |
| Feb | Tours 2-4 places, negotiates terms, checks with current/former tenants | Empowered |
| Mar | Signs lease; may overlap with current lease | Resignation or satisfaction |

#### Information Sources
- **Primary:** Personal experience and peer network (strongest signal)
- **Secondary:** Reddit (r/[university] housing threads), GroupMe/Discord servers
- **Tertiary:** Google reviews of specific properties, Apartments.com for new listings
- **Trust hierarchy:** Own experience > friend testimonials > online reviews > landlord claims

#### Pain Points (Ranked by Severity)

1. **Landlord unaccountability** -- Has experienced or heard horror stories; "Off campus student housing is a literal nightmare" (Spectator student newspaper); mold, unresponsive maintenance, deposit theft
2. **Rent inflation** -- Renewal offers above market with no justification; feeling of being "bled" by landlords "bleeding money"
3. **No pricing transparency** -- Impossible to know if rent is fair relative to comparable units
4. **Lease trap dynamics** -- Early signing deadlines create artificial urgency; renewal pressure
5. **Maintenance neglect** -- "hot days without Air Conditioning and the prominent smell of 'old house'"

#### Decision Criteria (Ranked)
1. Landlord/management quality (learned the hard way)
2. Price relative to market (wants a deal, not just cheap)
3. Location (knows exact neighborhoods)
4. Specific unit condition (not fooled by photos)
5. Lease flexibility (subletting clause, break options)
6. Amenities

#### CampusNest Value
**Primary pillar: Landlord Reputation System** -- This persona will be the most prolific reviewer and the most avid consumer of reviews. They have stories to tell and want to warn others.

**Secondary pillar: Housing Intelligence Engine** -- Fairness scoring and comparable pricing give them negotiation leverage with landlords.

#### Adoption Trigger
Receives a renewal offer with a 15% rent increase. Searches for "is $X fair for [neighborhood]?" and finds CampusNest's fairness scorer. Then discovers the landlord review system and spends 30 minutes reading (and writing) reviews.

### Persona 3: The Transfer Student

**Name archetype:** "Transplant Taylor"

#### Demographics
- **Age:** 19-22
- **Year:** Sophomore or Junior
- **Budget:** $650-$1,000/month
- **Living situation:** Currently at community college or different university; may know zero people at new school
- **Financial literacy:** Variable; may have rental experience from prior school

#### Housing Journey (Timeline: Compressed -- 4-8 weeks)

| Week | Step | Emotion |
|------|------|---------|
| 1 | Accepted transfer; immediately realizes housing is urgent | Panic |
| 2 | Searches online from remote location; can't visit in person | Helplessness |
| 3 | Reaches out to transfer student groups on social media | Cautious hope |
| 4-5 | Virtual tours, phone calls with landlords; makes decision with limited info | Anxiety, leap of faith |
| 6 | Signs lease sight-unseen (common); arranges move | Dread + excitement |
| 8 | Moves in; discovers reality vs. expectations | Relief or regret |

#### Information Sources
- **Primary:** University transfer student office, online listings (Apartments.com, Zillow)
- **Secondary:** Facebook/Reddit groups for the specific university
- **Tertiary:** Google Street View (remote area assessment), YouTube campus vlogs
- **Trust hierarchy:** Transfer student peers > university resources > online listings > landlord websites

#### Pain Points (Ranked by Severity)

1. **Compressed timeline** -- "Winter semester presents unique challenges, with fewer students departing during December and available units tending toward less desirable options"
2. **No local network** -- Cannot tap into word-of-mouth that returning students rely on
3. **Remote decision-making** -- Must often sign sight-unseen; high scam risk (15% of students contact scammers without realizing it)
4. **Lease-academic calendar mismatch** -- May transfer mid-year; leases rarely align with semester start
5. **Roommate uncertainty** -- Finding compatible strangers from a distance is difficult
6. **Neighborhood ignorance** -- "If you aren't familiar with the area, you won't know where to look"

#### Decision Criteria (Ranked)
1. Availability (beggars can't be choosers in compressed timelines)
2. Proximity to campus (unfamiliar with area, defaults to close)
3. Price
4. Safety (especially for those moving sight-unseen)
5. Verified reviews/photos (anything to reduce uncertainty)
6. Roommate matching

#### CampusNest Value
**Primary pillar: Housing Intelligence Engine** -- Campus-specific neighborhood guides, verified photos, and real cost data are lifelines for someone making remote decisions.

**Secondary pillar: Verified Sublet/Roommate Marketplace** -- Roommate matching and verified listings reduce the risk of remote decision-making.

**Tertiary: Landlord Reputation System** -- Reviews from current students substitute for the local network they lack.

#### Adoption Trigger
Googles "[university name] off-campus housing guide" during the first week after acceptance. Finds CampusNest's campus-specific intelligence. The "verified" label on listings and landlord reviews gives them confidence to make a remote decision.

### Persona 4: The Graduate Student

**Name archetype:** "PhD Priya" / "Grad-Family Greg"

#### Demographics
- **Age:** 23-32
- **Year:** Masters or PhD (1-6 year programs)
- **Budget:** $900-$1,500/month (stipend-dependent; 34% of undergrads and 38% of grad students earn under $20,000/year)
- **Living situation:** May have partner, spouse, or children; needs 1-2 bedroom unit
- **Financial literacy:** Higher than undergrads but still limited by income

#### Housing Journey (Timeline: 2-4 months before program start)

| Month | Step | Emotion |
|-------|------|---------|
| -4 | Accepted to program; begins remote research on area | Methodical |
| -3 | Contacts department peers, checks university grad housing waitlist | Frustration (long waitlists) |
| -2 | Searches broader rental market; factors in partner's commute, school districts if kids | Overwhelmed by competing constraints |
| -1 | Visits campus; tours 3-6 places; signs lease | Pragmatic compromise |
| 0 | Moves in; settles for multi-year stay | Acceptance |

#### Information Sources
- **Primary:** Department peers (existing grad students), university grad housing office
- **Secondary:** General rental platforms (Zillow, Apartments.com, Craigslist)
- **Tertiary:** Google Maps (commute modeling), neighborhood crime data, school ratings
- **Trust hierarchy:** Department peers > university resources > Zillow/Apartments.com > landlord marketing

#### Pain Points (Ranked by Severity)

1. **Affordability on stipend** -- "Graduate students struggle with high rents" (Maryland Matters); stipends rarely keep pace with housing inflation
2. **Multi-year commitment risk** -- Signing 12-month leases with uncertain funding/program continuation
3. **Partner/family constraints** -- Need to optimize for two commutes, possibly school districts; university housing waitlists can be 1-2 years for family units
4. **Age/lifestyle mismatch** -- Don't want undergrad party neighborhoods but priced out of professional neighborhoods
5. **Limited university support** -- Grad housing offices are understaffed; "more than half of surveyed institutions operate with just 1 to 10 professional housing staff members"

#### Decision Criteria (Ranked)
1. Price relative to stipend (hard budget constraint)
2. Lease length and flexibility (may need to leave if funding ends)
3. Commute to campus/lab
4. Neighborhood character (quiet, safe, possibly family-friendly)
5. Unit size (needs space for partner/family/home office)
6. Pet policy

#### CampusNest Value
**Primary pillar: Housing Intelligence Engine** -- Cost calculator calibrated to grad stipend, commute optimization, and neighborhood intelligence help navigate the unique constraints.

**Secondary pillar: Landlord Reputation System** -- Multi-year leases make landlord quality even more critical; a bad landlord for 4 years of a PhD is devastating.

#### Adoption Trigger
New admit joins department Slack/Discord and asks "where should I live?" An existing grad student shares a CampusNest link with the neighborhood guide and cost comparison, saying "this saved me thousands."

### Persona 5: The International Student

**Name archetype:** "Visa Vikram" / "Global Grace"

#### Demographics
- **Age:** 18-28 (undergrad and grad)
- **Year:** Any, but most acute for new arrivals
- **Budget:** $700-$1,200/month (varies widely; some well-funded, others very constrained)
- **Living situation:** Arriving alone to unfamiliar country; may need furnished unit
- **Financial literacy (US):** Very low -- unfamiliar with US rental system, credit, co-signers

#### Housing Journey (Timeline: 1-3 months before arrival, often remote)

| Month | Step | Emotion |
|-------|------|---------|
| -3 | Receives visa; begins housing search from home country | Confusion, culture shock |
| -2 | Discovers US rental system requires credit history, SSN, co-signer -- none of which they have | Frustration, anxiety |
| -1 | Explores university housing (waitlisted), contacts international student office, joins WhatsApp/WeChat groups from home country peers | Desperate networking |
| 0 | Arrives; may stay in temporary housing (Airbnb, hotel) while searching in person | Overwhelming stress |
| +1 | Signs lease with extra deposit or guarantor service; moves in | Relief but financial strain |

#### Information Sources
- **Primary:** Home country peer networks (WeChat, WhatsApp, KakaoTalk groups)
- **Secondary:** University international student office, university housing website
- **Tertiary:** Facebook groups, general listing platforms
- **Trust hierarchy:** Home country peers who've done it > international student office > any other source

#### Pain Points (Ranked by Severity)

1. **No US credit history/SSN** -- "International students that are coming to the US typically have no US credit history or FICO score, which makes it really difficult for operators to assess credit worthiness" (RentBottomLine)
2. **Remote search from abroad** -- Time zone differences, can't visit, high scam vulnerability; "more than 98% of those students [who contacted scammers] losing money"
3. **Guarantor requirement** -- Most landlords require US-based co-signer; expensive third-party guarantor services ($300-$600)
4. **Cultural barriers** -- Unfamiliar with lease terms, tenant rights, utility setup, deposit norms
5. **Furnished housing scarcity** -- Most US apartments are unfurnished; international students often can't bring or buy furniture easily
6. **Visa-linked housing anxiety** -- Visa uncertainty affects willingness to sign long leases; "visa uncertainty threatens student housing occupancy"

#### Decision Criteria (Ranked)
1. Accepts international students (no SSN/credit requirement)
2. Furnished or near IKEA/furniture access
3. Price
4. Safety (parents especially concerned about unfamiliar country)
5. Proximity to campus and grocery stores (may not have car)
6. Community (preference for buildings with other international students)

#### CampusNest Value
**Primary pillar: Housing Intelligence Engine** -- Filtering by "international-student friendly" (no SSN required, furnished, guarantor alternatives) solves the #1 pain point directly.

**Secondary pillar: Landlord Reputation System** -- International students are most vulnerable to scams and landlord exploitation; verified reviews from other international students are invaluable.

**Tertiary: Verified Sublet/Roommate Marketplace** -- Finding same-culture or same-language roommates through verified channels reduces risk.

#### Adoption Trigger
A student in their home country peer WhatsApp group shares a CampusNest link, saying "this shows which landlords accept international students without a co-signer." The filter alone is enough to convert.

### Persona 6: The Mid-Lease Subletter

**Name archetype:** "Co-op Connor" / "Abroad Aisha"

#### Demographics
- **Age:** 19-23
- **Year:** Sophomore-Senior
- **Budget:** N/A (they're trying to offload cost, not spend)
- **Living situation:** Has active lease but leaving for co-op, study abroad, or internship for 3-6 months
- **Financial literacy:** Moderate; understands lease obligations but may not know subletting rules

#### Housing Journey (Timeline: Compressed -- 2-6 weeks to find subtenant)

| Week | Step | Emotion |
|------|------|---------|
| 1 | Accepted to study abroad/co-op; realizes they'll be paying rent on empty apartment | Financial dread |
| 2 | Checks lease for subletting clause; asks landlord for permission | Bureaucratic frustration |
| 3 | Posts on every platform they can think of (Facebook groups, GroupMe, Reddit, Craigslist) | Desperation |
| 4-5 | Fields inquiries from strangers; tries to vet them with zero tools | Anxiety about liability |
| 6 | Signs informal sublet agreement (often not legally robust); hands over keys | Lingering worry |

#### Information Sources
- **Primary:** University Facebook housing groups, friends-of-friends
- **Secondary:** Reddit, Craigslist, university housing board
- **Tertiary:** Semester Sublet and similar niche platforms
- **Trust hierarchy:** Friends > friends-of-friends > verified strangers > anonymous internet strangers

#### Pain Points (Ranked by Severity)

1. **Time pressure** -- Need to fill spot before departure; "it's recommended to start the process by mid-November or at least 45 days before the sublet should begin"
2. **Continued liability** -- "As the original tenant listed on the lease contract, you remain jointly and severally liable for the rent and for maintaining the unit"
3. **Vetting difficulty** -- No reliable way to evaluate unknown subtenants
4. **Pricing uncertainty** -- Don't know what to charge; often underpriced out of desperation or overpriced and get no takers
5. **Lease restriction complexity** -- Many leases prohibit or restrict subletting; navigating landlord approval is opaque
6. **No legal template** -- Most students create informal, unenforceable sublet agreements

#### Decision Criteria (Ranked)
1. Speed of finding subtenant (time is the enemy)
2. Reliability/trustworthiness of subtenant
3. Price recovery (maximize rent coverage)
4. Legal protection (sublet agreement quality)
5. Ease of process (don't want to manage from abroad)

#### CampusNest Value
**Primary pillar: Verified Sublet/Roommate Marketplace** -- This is the exact use case. Verified student profiles, standardized sublet agreements, and a campus-specific marketplace directly solve the core problem.

**Secondary pillar: Housing Intelligence Engine** -- Pricing guidance (what's a fair sublet rate for this unit) helps both subletter and subtenant.

#### Adoption Trigger
Posts in a Facebook group "looking for someone to sublet my room Jan-May." Gets 15 DMs from strangers with no way to vet them. A friend says "just list it on CampusNest -- they verify students and have a sublet agreement template."

## 4. Validation Experiment Designs

### Experiment 1: Returning Upperclassman -- Landlord Review Landing Page Test

**Hypothesis:** Upperclassmen who have had negative landlord experiences will engage with a "Rate Your Landlord" landing page at 2x the rate of a generic housing search page.

**Method:**
- Create two landing pages: (A) "Find apartments near [University]" vs. (B) "Your landlord's reputation score: see what other students say"
- Target via Reddit ads in university subreddits (r/[school]) during renewal season (Nov-Feb)
- Measure: click-through, time-on-page, email signup, review submission

**Channel:** Reddit ads, university subreddit organic posts
**Budget:** $500 for 2-week test across 3 campuses
**Success metric:** >5% email signup rate on version B; >20 organic reviews submitted per campus in first 2 weeks

**Partnership opportunity:** Student government associations (like UCSB's A.S. Rate My Landlord -- launched Feb 2026 -- proving demand exists)

---

### Experiment 2: Returning Upperclassman -- Campus Ambassador Content Creation

**Hypothesis:** Student ambassadors creating "apartment tour + honest review" TikTok/Instagram content will drive 10x more engagement than static listings.

**Method:**
- Recruit 3-5 upperclassmen per campus as ambassadors ($50/month or housing credit)
- Each creates 2-4 video reviews/month of local apartments and landlords
- Tag CampusNest, use campus-specific hashtags
- Track: views, profile visits, signups attributed to ambassador content

**Channel:** TikTok, Instagram Reels
**Budget:** $250/month per campus (ambassador stipends)
**Success metric:** >1,000 views per video average; >50 signups per campus per month

---

### Experiment 3: Mid-Lease Subletter -- Urgency Micro-Survey

**Hypothesis:** Students going on study abroad or co-op will report subletting as a top-3 stress source and express willingness to pay for a verified subletting platform.

**Method:**
- 5-question survey distributed via study abroad offices and co-op program coordinators
  1. "How stressed are you about subletting your apartment?" (1-10)
  2. "How are you currently finding a subtenant?" (multi-select)
  3. "How much would you pay for a service that verifies subtenants and provides a legal sublet agreement?" ($0 / $25 / $50 / $75)
  4. "Have you or someone you know had a bad subletting experience?" (Y/N + optional description)
  5. "Would you use a campus-specific subletting marketplace?" (Y/N)

**Channel:** Study abroad pre-departure orientations, co-op program emails
**Budget:** $0 (partnership-driven distribution)
**Success metric:** >100 responses; >60% report stress level 7+; >40% willing to pay $25+

---

### Experiment 4: Transfer Student -- "New to [University]" Search Intent Capture

**Hypothesis:** Transfer students actively search "[university] off-campus housing guide" within 2 weeks of acceptance and will convert on a campus-specific landing page.

**Method:**
- Create SEO-optimized landing pages: "[University Name] Transfer Student Housing Guide"
- Include: neighborhood map, average rent by area, "verified listings," roommate finder CTA
- Run Google Ads on "[university] transfer housing" and "[university] apartments" keywords
- Measure: organic + paid traffic, time-on-page, email signup, listing clicks

**Channel:** Google Search (SEO + SEM)
**Budget:** $300/month per campus for Google Ads
**Success metric:** >500 organic monthly visits per campus page within 3 months; >8% email signup conversion

---

### Experiment 5: International Student -- WhatsApp/WeChat Group Seeding

**Hypothesis:** International students trust peer recommendations in home-country messaging groups more than any other channel, and a single trusted share will drive >20 signups.

**Method:**
- Partner with international student organizations at 3 pilot campuses
- Identify incoming student WhatsApp/WeChat groups by nationality (Chinese, Indian, Korean -- largest cohorts)
- Have current international students (ambassadors) share CampusNest link with personal testimonial
- Create "International Student Housing Checklist" as lead magnet (covers SSN alternatives, guarantor services, furnished options)

**Channel:** WhatsApp, WeChat, KakaoTalk groups
**Budget:** $100/campus (ambassador stipends + checklist design)
**Success metric:** >20 signups per group share; >50% of signups use "international-friendly" filter within first session

**Partnership opportunity:** International student offices, cultural student associations, international orientation programs

---

### Experiment 6: Incoming Freshman -- Parent-Targeted Social Proof Test

**Hypothesis:** Parents making housing decisions with their freshman will respond to safety/transparency messaging and landlord accountability features.

**Method:**
- Create parent-oriented landing page: "Help Your Student Find Safe, Fairly-Priced Housing Near [University]"
- Emphasize: verified landlord reviews, true cost calculator, safety scores
- Distribute via parent Facebook groups, university parent newsletters, admitted student events
- A/B test messaging: (A) "Save money" vs. (B) "Stay safe and informed"

**Channel:** Facebook (parent groups), university parent communications
**Budget:** $400 for Facebook ads targeting parents of college-age students
**Success metric:** >3% click-through on parent-targeted ads; >100 parent email signups per campus

---

### Experiment 7: Graduate Student -- Department Slack/Discord Infiltration

**Hypothesis:** Graduate students will adopt CampusNest if it's recommended in their department communication channels by current grad students.

**Method:**
- Identify 5 large graduate departments per pilot campus (CS, Engineering, Business, Biology, Education)
- Recruit one "grad housing champion" per department
- Champion posts CampusNest link in department Slack/Discord with personal endorsement during admit season (Feb-Apr)
- Track signups with department-specific referral codes

**Channel:** Department Slack, Discord, email lists
**Budget:** $50/champion (gift card)
**Success metric:** >15 signups per department post; >30% of signups use cost calculator within first session

## 5. Flywheel Strategy & Launch Sequence

### The CampusNest Flywheel

```
Upperclassmen write landlord reviews
        |
        v
Review content attracts transfer + international students (SEO)
        |
        v
New users search listings using Housing Intelligence
        |
        v
Subletters list on marketplace (study abroad/co-op departures)
        |
        v
Marketplace listings attract MORE transfer + international students
        |
        v
Growing user base = more reviews = better data = more trust
        |
        v
Freshmen + parents adopt because platform is now "the standard"
        |
        v
Landlords engage (claim profiles, respond to reviews, list units)
        |
        v
Supply-side revenue unlocked
```

### Phase 1: Seed (Months 1-3) -- 2-3 Pilot Campuses

**Target personas:** Returning Upperclassmen + Mid-Lease Subletters
**Goal:** 500+ landlord reviews, 50+ sublet listings per campus
**Tactics:**
- Campus ambassador program (5 per campus)
- Partner with student government for landlord review drive (UCSB model: water filter giveaway for reviews)
- Launch sublet marketplace timed to spring study-abroad departures (Jan-Feb)
- SEO content: "[University] landlord reviews," "[University] apartment ratings"

**Key metric:** Review velocity (reviews/week) -- this is the atomic unit of the flywheel

### Phase 2: Attract (Months 4-6) -- Expand to 5-8 Campuses

**Target personas:** Transfer Students + International Students
**Goal:** 1,000+ monthly active users per campus
**Tactics:**
- Partner with transfer student offices and international student offices
- Launch "International Student Housing Guide" per campus
- Google Ads on "[University] transfer housing" keywords
- WhatsApp/WeChat group seeding for international cohorts
- Deploy roommate matching feature

**Key metric:** Organic traffic growth (monthly unique visitors from search)

### Phase 3: Scale (Months 7-12) -- 15-20 Campuses

**Target personas:** Incoming Freshmen + Graduate Students
**Goal:** Platform is "the standard" at pilot campuses; 5,000+ MAU per campus
**Tactics:**
- University housing office partnerships (official resource listing)
- Parent-targeted campaigns during admitted student events (Feb-Apr)
- Grad department Slack infiltration
- Begin landlord-side features (claim profile, respond to reviews, premium listings)
- Expand to adjacent campuses in same metro areas

**Key metric:** Net Promoter Score + repeat usage rate

| Geographic expansion after proving one vertical | Campus-by-campus expansion after proving pilot markets | HIGH -- "go niche, then expand" per a16z playbook |

### Estimated Persona Acquisition Costs

| Persona | Organic CAC | Paid CAC | Strategy |
|---------|-------------|----------|----------|
| Returning Upperclassman | $2-5 (ambassador + word-of-mouth) | $15-25 (Reddit/Instagram ads) | Organic-first; ambassador program is primary driver |
| Mid-Lease Subletter | $0-3 (study abroad office partnership) | $10-20 (targeted ads) | Partnership-driven; near-zero CAC through institutional channels |
| Transfer Student | $5-10 (SEO content) | $20-40 (Google Ads on high-intent keywords) | SEO-first; Google Ads for immediate capture |
| International Student | $3-8 (peer network seeding) | $25-45 (targeted social ads) | Peer-network-first; viral coefficient is highest of all personas |
| Incoming Freshman | $8-15 (parent channels + orientation) | $30-50 (Facebook parent targeting) | Most expensive; requires institutional partnerships |
| Graduate Student | $5-12 (department channels) | $20-35 (LinkedIn/targeted) | Department infiltration is most efficient channel |

**Industry benchmark:** Higher education B2B CAC averages $1,143. CampusNest's B2C student acquisition should target $5-15 organic CAC, leveraging campus networks and peer virality -- dramatically below industry average by focusing on organic/community channels.

### Strategic Recommendation

**Start demand-side, campus-by-campus, content-first.** The a16z playbook is clear: "whether you start on the demand side, the supply side, or a limited version of both, it's best to focus on the most constrained aspect of the market." For student housing, the constrained side is **trustworthy information** -- not listings. Students can find listings on Zillow and Apartments.com. What they cannot find is:

1. Whether a landlord will actually fix the heat
2. Whether $950/month is fair for a 2BR near campus
3. Whether this Craigslist sublet is a scam

CampusNest's moat is the intelligence and reputation data that no horizontal platform will ever invest in building campus-by-campus. This is the Niche.com playbook applied to housing: build the review/data layer first, attract users with free information, then monetize the supply side once you own the demand.
