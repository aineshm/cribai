# CampusNest Expansion Playbook: Market Research & Demand Analysis

**Prepared:** March 2026
**Scope:** 18 target expansion markets beyond UW-Madison
**Methodology:** Data-driven, demand-first ranking

---

## 1. Executive Summary

CampusNest has validated its product at UW-Madison. This playbook identifies and ranks 18 expansion markets based on five key criteria: student housing pain intensity, off-campus population size, competitive landscape weakness, market data accessibility, and rental market tightness.

**Key findings:**
- 78% of U.S. college students live off-campus, yet no dominant platform exists for student-specific rental intelligence
- National off-campus occupancy hit 95.1% for the 2025-2026 academic year -- one of the strongest performances in recent years
- Markets with the highest pain (Virginia Tech, Michigan, UIUC, Georgia Tech) combine low vacancy, rapidly rising rents, and documented housing crises
- Existing platforms (Apartments.com, Zillow, Niche) lack student-specific intelligence: lease term transparency, landlord ratings, fairness scoring, and AI-driven comparison
- The demand-first playbook (launch where pain is highest, build demand-side value, let supply follow) is validated by Niche.com's own trajectory

**Recommended first wave:** 5 Tier 1 markets launched over 12 months, followed by 7 Tier 2 markets in months 12-24.

## 4. Tier 1 Market Briefs

---

### 4.1 Virginia Tech -- Blacksburg, VA

**Why #1:** Blacksburg has the lowest vacancy rate of any college town in the nation. Students sign leases 9-10 months in advance. The Board of Visitors recently voted to cancel a 5,000-bed campus housing expansion, pushing even more demand onto the private market. This is a textbook supply-constrained, high-pain market.

**Key Statistics**
| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Total enrollment | ~38,000 |
| On-campus beds | ~9,500 |
| Est. off-campus students | ~29,000 |
| Average rent (per bed) | ~$1,250/month |
| Preleasing velocity | Highest in the nation (per Yardi) |
| Vacancy rate | Lowest in the U.S. among college towns |
| YoY rent growth | Strong upward pressure; 8-story, 759-bed project approved Mar 2025 |

**Top 3 Landlords / Property Managers**
1. **Landmark Properties** -- Operates The Station at Blacksburg, major purpose-built student housing
2. **VERVE (Greystar)** -- New development, modern amenities, aggressive preleasing
3. **CMG Leasing** -- Dominant local PM, manages hundreds of units in Blacksburg

**Primary Competitor & Weakness**
- **College Pads** (official VT partner) -- Basic listing aggregator with no intelligence layer. No lease term analysis, no landlord ratings, no price fairness scoring. Students use it because VT links to it, not because it solves their problem.
- Students also rely on **Facebook groups** and word of mouth, which are unreliable and opaque.

**Recommended Launch Strategy**
1. Partner with the Collegiate Times (student newspaper) for awareness; leverage the BOV housing cancellation as a news hook
2. Scrape Apartments.com, Zillow, and local PM sites for Blacksburg/Christiansburg listings
3. Lead with CribAI's lease term explainer -- Virginia tenant law is complex and students signing 10-month-early leases need protection
4. Target the October-November signing season with a "Don't sign blind" campaign

### 4.2 University of Michigan -- Ann Arbor, MI

**Why #2:** Record enrollment growth (up 40% since 2000), a decades-long housing shortage, and a bifurcated market where students choose between overpriced luxury ($3,000/mo) and outdated walkups. Only freshmen get guaranteed housing. Rents rose 35% from 2016-2023, far outpacing national averages.

**Key Statistics**
| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Total enrollment | ~48,000 |
| On-campus beds | ~10,000 (freshmen priority) |
| Est. off-campus students | ~35,000 |
| 1BR average rent | $2,500-$3,000/month |
| Returning housing applications | 2,800 for ~1,100 slots |
| Rent growth (2016-2023) | +35% (vs. 24% national) |

**Top 3 Landlords / Property Managers**
1. **McKinley Inc.** -- Largest apartment manager in Ann Arbor, thousands of units
2. **Oxford Companies** -- Major local commercial and residential PM
3. **Greystar / Hub** -- Purpose-built student housing near campus

**Primary Competitor & Weakness**
- **Apartments.com / Zillow** -- Generic platforms with no student-specific features. No roommate matching, no walkability-to-campus scoring, no lease fairness analysis.
- **Michigan Daily classifieds** -- Informal, unverified, no structured data.

**Recommended Launch Strategy**
1. Target the February-March leasing season when returning students scramble
2. Build a "Price Fairness" feature comparing luxury vs. value listings with walking distance tradeoffs
3. Partner with student government (Central Student Government) which has advocated for housing transparency
4. Scrape Ann Arbor rental data; landlord concentration makes comprehensive coverage feasible

### 4.3 University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign

**Why #3:** Over 20,000 students live in private off-campus housing. The university suffered a housing crisis in 2024 with insufficient rooms for admitted students, spilling demand into an already competitive Campustown market. Green Street Realty alone manages 2,000+ units, indicating PM concentration that aids scraping coverage.

**Key Statistics**
| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Total enrollment | ~56,000 |
| On-campus beds | ~10,000 |
| Est. off-campus students | ~42,000 |
| Campustown rent range | $700-$1,200/month |
| Downtown Urbana rent | $600-$1,000/month |
| Market condition | Seller's market since 2023 |

**Top 3 Landlords / Property Managers**
1. **Green Street Realty** -- 2,000+ units, largest in Champaign-Urbana
2. **Bankier Apartments** -- Long-established local PM with hundreds of properties
3. **JSM Living** -- Manages student-focused properties in Campustown area

**Primary Competitor & Weakness**
- **College Pads** and **Apartments.com** -- Listing-only; no AI-driven comparison, no cost calculator adjusted for Champaign's utility structures, no landlord reputation data.
- The Daily Illini has editorialized about the crisis, indicating unmet information needs.

**Recommended Launch Strategy**
1. Scrape Green Street Realty, Bankier, and JSM listings for near-complete Campustown coverage
2. Lead with cost-calculator feature: Champaign utilities vary dramatically by building age
3. Partner with Daily Illini for co-branded housing guide
4. Target September-November pre-leasing window (houses) and January-March (apartments)

### 4.4 Georgia Tech -- Atlanta, GA

**Why #4:** GT provides housing for ~8,000 students, but ~15,000 others need off-campus housing in one of the most expensive urban rental markets in the Southeast. Off-campus rents start at $1,800/month near campus. The metro Atlanta housing crisis compounds the student-specific shortage.

**Key Statistics**
| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Total enrollment | ~45,000 |
| On-campus beds | ~8,000 |
| Est. off-campus students | ~15,000+ (undergrad) + grad students |
| Near-campus rent | $1,200-$2,000+ per bed/month |
| Market context | Atlanta metro housing crisis backdrop |
| Preleasing velocity | Among highest nationally (Yardi Jan 2026) |

**Top 3 Landlords / Property Managers**
1. **Greystar** -- Multiple properties in Midtown Atlanta near GT
2. **American Campus Communities** -- Purpose-built student housing near campus
3. **Rambler Atlanta** -- Student-focused off-campus housing brand

**Primary Competitor & Weakness**
- **College Pads** (official GT partner) -- Same limitations as VT: listing aggregator without intelligence. GT's OCP marketplace is functional but basic.
- Atlanta's general rental platforms (Zillow, etc.) don't filter for GT-specific needs like MARTA access, campus shuttle routes, or GT meal plan compatibility.

**Recommended Launch Strategy**
1. Focus on the Midtown/Home Park/West Midtown neighborhoods that GT students actually target
2. Build transit-aware search: MARTA access and GT Stinger shuttle routes are critical differentiators
3. Partner with the Technique (student newspaper) and GT Student Government
4. Lean into the lease explainer tool -- Georgia landlord-tenant law is highly landlord-friendly

### 4.5 NC State -- Raleigh, NC

**Why #5:** NC State has ~11,000 beds for nearly 30,000 undergrads. In Spring 2025, 2,400 students who applied for on-campus housing were denied. The university is actively growing enrollment while housing supply lags. Raleigh's booming tech economy compounds rental pressure.

**Key Statistics**
| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Total enrollment | ~37,000 |
| On-campus beds | ~11,000 |
| Off-campus percentage | ~62% |
| Students denied on-campus (Spring 2025) | ~2,400 |
| Raleigh rental market | Tight; tech sector driving demand |
| Key neighborhoods | Hillsborough St., Western Blvd., Avent Ferry |

**Top 3 Landlords / Property Managers**
1. **Landmark Properties** -- The Station Raleigh, large purpose-built community
2. **Greystar** -- Multiple Raleigh student properties
3. **Valentine Properties** -- Valentine Commons, established off-campus high-rise

**Primary Competitor & Weakness**
- NC State's own off-campus housing portal is basic (listing board only)
- **College Pads** and general platforms dominate but lack Raleigh-specific intelligence
- No platform addresses the Wolfline bus route optimization that NC State students depend on

**Recommended Launch Strategy**
1. Build Wolfline transit integration -- students heavily use the campus bus system for off-campus commuting
2. Target the March-April window when denied on-campus students scramble
3. Partner with Technician (student newspaper) for housing crisis coverage tie-ins
4. Scrape Landmark, Greystar, and local PM sites; Raleigh market data is highly accessible

## 5. Tier 2 Market Summaries

### 5.1 University of Florida -- Gainesville, FL
**Score: 80 | Blocking factor: Existing platform saturation**

- 52,000 enrollment, 75% off-campus (~39,000 students)
- Rent growth of 8.5% YoY (among highest nationally)
- Gainesville is a classic college town with concentrated demand
- **Blocker:** Multiple established platforms (SwampRentals, etc.) already serve this market. Competition is present but undifferentiated -- opportunity exists for intelligence layer
- Strong market fundamentals; move to Tier 1 if competitive analysis reveals weak spots

### 5.2 University of Texas at Austin
**Score: 78 | Blocking factor: Market size and complexity**

- 54,000 enrollment, 83% off-campus (~45,000 students)
- West Campus is the densest student housing market in the country
- New 30-story Icon tower (555 beds) and $145M Balfour Beatty project signal massive demand
- Median asking rents dropped ~9% (May 2024-May 2025) due to new supply, creating a window
- **Blocker:** Austin is an enormous, complex metro market. Requires significant scraping infrastructure. Consider West Campus micro-launch.

### 5.3 University of Tennessee -- Knoxville, TN
**Score: 77 | Blocking factor: New supply entering market**

- 36,000 enrollment with aggressive growth trajectory
- Documented housing crunch: students scrambling, rents skyrocketing
- Multiple new developments (VERVE, Hub, The Davy) entering 2025-2026
- **Blocker:** New supply may temporarily ease pressure; 28 schools were >10% behind prior year's leasing pace (UTK among them per Yardi)
- Monitor for 6 months; if new supply doesn't meet demand, escalate to Tier 1

### 5.4 Clemson University -- Clemson, SC
**Score: 76 | Blocking factor: Small town market size**

- 28,000 enrollment, ~70% off-campus
- Rent growth among the fastest in the nation (Wall Street Journal report)
- Campus View Apartments: rent up 66% from $950 to $1,575 (2020-2025)
- Students must start housing search by October for fall
- **Blocker:** Small market size limits total addressable user base. Strong pain signals but limited scale.

### 5.5 Penn State -- State College, PA
**Score: 74 | Blocking factor: Relatively stable market**

- 47,000 enrollment, 66% off-campus (~31,000 students)
- 97.3% preleased (highest among Power 5 schools)
- State College is isolated -- students have no alternative but local market
- **Blocker:** Market appears functional despite tightness. Less crisis-level pain documented compared to Tier 1 markets. Strong fundamentals for future launch.

### 5.6 Purdue University -- West Lafayette, IN
**Score: 73 | Blocking factor: New supply pressure**

- 50,000 enrollment, ~65% off-campus
- 2,556 apartments available near campus
- Multiple new luxury developments (VERVE, Lark, Redpoint)
- **Blocker:** Purdue was among 28 schools >10% behind prior year leasing (Yardi Jan 2026). New supply is weighing on the market. Good long-term target; wait for supply absorption.

### 5.7 University of Colorado Boulder
**Score: 72 | Blocking factor: High rent baseline limits student audience**

- 38,000 enrollment, ~23,000 upper-division students off-campus
- On-campus capacity: ~8,700 beds (7,200 freshmen, 1,500 upperclassmen)
- Board of Regents advancing major property acquisition (Aug 2025) to expand housing
- Boulder consistently ranks among most expensive CO rental markets
- **Blocker:** Extremely high rents ($2,500+ for 1BR) may limit CampusNest's value proposition for budget-constrained students. The market is tight but students may prioritize price over intelligence.

## 6. Tier 3 Market Summaries

### 6.1 Ohio State University -- Columbus, OH
**Score: 68** -- Massive enrollment (61,000) but new sophomore living requirement is shifting demand patterns. Market may become less competitive for off-campus housing as more students stay in dorms for two years. Monitor policy impact through 2026.

### 6.2 Texas A&M -- College Station, TX
**Score: 67** -- 68,000 enrollment with ~40,000 off-campus, but Bryan-College Station has abundant supply and relatively affordable rents. Less acute pain signal than other markets. Good scale play for later phases.

### 6.3 Michigan State -- East Lansing, MI
**Score: 65** -- 50,000 enrollment, well-established off-campus ecosystem with DTN/CCM managing 300+ properties for 50+ years. Market is mature and well-served by local operators. Lower disruption opportunity.

### 6.4 Auburn University -- Auburn, AL
**Score: 63** -- 34,000 enrollment with strong preleasing (per Yardi). Purpose-built student housing market is robust. Auburn-Opelika community has adequate supply. Limited crisis signals compared to Tier 1/2 markets.

### 6.5 University of Central Florida -- Orlando, FL
**Score: 62** -- Largest U.S. university by enrollment (69,000) but sprawling campus in a car-dependent metro. Students dispersed across wide geography. Not a traditional "college town" dynamic, which reduces the concentrated pain that CampusNest addresses best. Revisit if product expands to metro/commuter models.

### 6.6 University of Missouri -- Columbia, MO
**Score: 58** -- Declining enrollment in recent years has created a renter-friendly market with abundant options. Average rent near Mizzou is $1,221/month (well below national average). Studio apartments average $479/month. Low pain environment. Monitor for enrollment recovery.

## 8. Competitive Landscape Summary

### Current Student Housing Search Ecosystem

| Platform | Strengths | Weaknesses | CampusNest Advantage |
|----------|-----------|------------|---------------------|
| Apartments.com | Massive inventory, good UX | Generic; no student intelligence | AI-driven comparison, lease explainer, fairness score |
| Zillow | Brand recognition, map search | Not student-focused at all | Campus-centric search, roommate-aware pricing |
| College Pads | University partnerships (VT, GT, Clemson) | Basic listing board, no intelligence | Full AI agent, landlord ratings, cost calculator |
| Niche.com | School rankings, review data | Housing is afterthought; reviews biased | Purpose-built for housing decisions, not school selection |
| Facebook Groups | Free, peer-driven | Unverified, scam-prone, no structure | Verified listings, transparent pricing, legal protection |
| Uloop | College-specific classifieds | Outdated UX, thin listings | Modern UX, AI-powered recommendations |
| AmberStudent | Price match, 24/7 support | International student focus | Domestic market depth, local knowledge |

### CampusNest's Differentiated Value

1. **CribAI Agent** -- No competitor offers an AI agent that can search, compare, explain lease terms, and schedule tours in a single conversational interface
2. **Fairness Scoring** -- Proprietary algorithm comparing listing prices to comparable properties
3. **Lease Term Knowledge Base** -- 28 lease terms explained with legal disclaimers, jurisdiction-specific
4. **Landlord Intelligence** -- Aggregated ratings and review data from multiple sources
5. **Cost Calculator** -- True cost of living including utilities, parking, transit, and hidden fees
